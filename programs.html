The programs, in detail

Four programs of 6 to 12 weeks, timed to your climate and the state of your colonies.

Program 01 · Set up properly

Your first hive

New beekeepers overspend on gadgets and underspend on the basics. We choose a hive type and stick with it, site it with sun, water and neighbors in mind, and walk through installation and the first six weeks, which is when most first colonies are lost.

  • 10 weeks
  • Before your bees arrive
  • 3 hours a week
  • Suit, smoker, tool

Package or nuc, hived and settled.

Building the habit of looking without wrecking things.

Weeks 1–2 · Eggs and pattern

Evidence of a laying queen, without hunting for her.

A full inspection in fifteen minutes.

Program 02 · What the frame says

Reading an inspection

The skill that separates second-year keepers from first-year ones is reading a frame quickly and putting it back. We work from your photos and video: spotting eggs without finding the queen, distinguishing swarm cells from supersedure, judging stores, and knowing when to close up and leave them alone.

  • 8 weeks
  • Have bees
  • 2 hours a week
  • A phone camera

Start this program

Program 03 · The thing that kills colonies

Varroa & colony health

Most winter losses trace back to mites nobody counted. We set up real monitoring with alcohol washes, plan treatment windows around the honey flow, and cover what foulbrood and other notifiable diseases look like — which is a call to your state apiary inspector, not a forum post.

  • 8 weeks
  • Have bees
  • 2 hours a week
  • Mite wash kit

Start this program

Weeks 1–2 · Counting

Alcohol wash technique and what the numbers mean.

Weeks 3–4 · Timing

Treatment windows around supers and brood cycles.

Weeks 5–6 · Labels and rules

Following product labels and local requirements.

Weeks 7–8 · Disease

What to recognize, and who to call.

Weeks 1–3 · Spring

Build-up, space and swarm prevention.

Weeks 4–6 · Flow

Supering and staying out of the way.

Weeks 7–9 · Harvest

Pulling, extracting and cleaning up.

Weeks 10–12 · Winter

Stores, mites, wraps and the last inspection.

Program 04 · Through the year

Seasons & harvest

We build a calendar for your zip code and flow: when to reverse, when to super, when to stop feeding, when to treat, when to leave them alone. Then harvest and extraction done cleanly, and the winter prep that decides whether you start next spring with bees.

  • 12 weeks
  • One season in
  • 3 hours a week
